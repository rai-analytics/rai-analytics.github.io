05Thesis research · 2026

When inflation hit 30%, who kept advertising?

My BBA thesis: 15 PSX-listed FMCG companies, six years, 90 firm-years of audited accounts. Every chart below runs on the published dataset.

0.008

The headline number

The correlation between inflation and ad spend across 90 firm-years. Basically zero.

Pearson r = 0.008, p = 0.943. When inflation hit 30.8%, firms did not move their ad budgets in step with it. Each firm followed its own balance sheet.

Pakistan's inflation and policy rate, 2019 to 2024

What the data shows

CPI inflation rose from 9.5% in 2021 to 30.8% in 2023. The average policy rate followed, reaching 20.6% in 2023. In the thesis data the two move together closely (r = 0.697, p < 0.001).

Cost pressure vs advertising

Average cost of goods sold as a share of revenue (bars) against the average advertising-to-sales ratio (line), across the 15 firms.

COGS ratio (left axis) Ad-to-sales (right axis) 2022 without Shield

What the data shows

Average ad-to-sales spiked to 6.4% in 2022, then was cut to 2.7% in 2023 as CPI peaked at 30.8%, and stayed near 2.6% in 2024.

One firm-year drives most of the 2022 spike: Shield Corporation reported advertising of 49.4% of revenue that year. Without it, the 2022 average is about 3.4%.

Did inflation move ad budgets?

Each dot is one firm in one year (90 firm-years). Across: that year's CPI inflation. Up: the firm's advertising-to-sales ratio. Dots in the same year are spread slightly sideways so they do not overlap.

One firm-year

Statistical result

Pearson correlation in SPSS across the 90 firm-years: r = 0.008, p = 0.943. There is no statistically significant linear relationship between inflation and ad spend at the firm level (p > 0.05). In the same year, firms ranged from almost nothing to over 10% of revenue: budgets followed each firm's own balance sheet strength, not the inflation rate.

Advertising Spend During Peak Inflation: An Analysis of Market Leaders in the Pakistani FMCG Sector (2019-2024)

How did 15 PSX-listed FMCG companies set their ad budgets through one of Pakistan's worst inflation periods? I pulled revenue, cost of goods sold and advertising expense out of the notes to six years of audited annual reports, cross-checked every figure into a 90-row dataset, built the ratios in Excel and tested them in SPSS.

15PSX-listed firms
6Years (2019-2024)
90Firm-years
PSXAnnual reports

Key finding

Inflation showed no statistically significant linear relationship with advertising spend at the firm level (p > 0.05). The sector average spiked to 6.4% of sales in 2022 and was cut to 2.7% in 2023, when CPI peaked at 30.8%. Budgets followed each firm's balance sheet strength, not a textbook response to inflation.

What I took from it

Averages lie politely. The sector line said "spike"; one firm said 49.4%. A good report shows both, and names the outlier.
